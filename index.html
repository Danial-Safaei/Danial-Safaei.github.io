01

Research

Virtual testing is now central to how autonomous vehicles are assured. The difficulty is that a simulator can be photorealistic and still be wrong in the only way that matters: the system under test may reach the same output for entirely different reasons.

My work argues that fidelity should be judged by decision evidence, not appearance. In Decisive Feature Fidelity (IEEE IV 2026) I introduced a system-specific metric that uses explainable-AI methods to compare the features actually driving a model's decisions across real and synthetic domains, together with estimators based on counterfactual explanations and a calibration scheme that measurably narrows the gap.

Before Warwick my research spanned trajectory prediction for human-centred smart environments and graph neural networks for biomedical discovery. The through-line is unchanged: methods that are interpretable, empirically grounded, and honest about what they do and do not establish.

Doctoral supervision

Supervised by Prof. Siddartha Khastgir and Prof. Matthew Higgins, WMG. Previously co-supervised by Dr Xingyu Zhao.

Industrial partner

Research collaboration with Siemens Digital Industries Software since February 2025.

Primary

Safety assurance for AI

How safety, reliability, and assurance claims can be argued with evidence when learned components operate in high-consequence settings.

Method

Explainable evaluation

Using interpretability methods as measurement instruments — comparing the evidence a model relies on, not only the outputs it produces.

Application

Synthetic-data fidelity

Establishing whether synthetic imagery is faithful to the decision-relevant structure of real data, so simulation results transfer.
