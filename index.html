Danial Safaei

PhD researcher, WMG, University of Warwick

I work on the safety assurance of AI-based perception for automated driving, and in particular on when simulation can be trusted as evidence. I am part of the Safe Autonomy Research Group at WMG, and my PhD is funded by EPSRC and Siemens.

I am supervised by Matthew Higgins, Thomas Bashford-Rogers and Siddartha Khastgir at WMG, and by Mohsen Alirezaei and Son Tong at Siemens Digital Industries Software. Before Warwick, I worked on deep learning and computer vision at the University of Tehran, where I completed an MSc in Computer Engineering (Artificial Intelligence and Robotics).

Research

Automated vehicles are increasingly tested in simulation. A synthetic image can look realistic, and a perception model can give the same output on it as on the matching real image, while relying on different evidence. My research measures this gap. I test whether a frozen model uses the same evidence on matched real and synthetic images, which we call mechanism parity, so that results from simulation can be weighed properly as safety evidence.
