01 Research

Measuring why a model decides, not just what it outputs.

Simulators and generative models now supply much of the evidence used to test camera-based perception in automated vehicles. But a model can give the same output on a real image and on its synthetic twin while relying on different visual evidence. My metrics compare that evidence, so a test passed in simulation can be trusted as evidence about the real world.

Instrument 01IEEE IV 2026

Decisive Feature Fidelity DFF

Uses counterfactual explanations to find the image evidence that decides a frozen model's output, then compares that evidence on a real frame and on its synthetic twin.

Instrument 02IEEE ITSC 2026

Concept-Decisive Feature Fidelity C-DFF

Maps the decisive evidence onto scene concepts such as road, cars, poles, buildings and sky, to show which parts of the scene a model relies on more, or less, in simulation.

How the measurement worksDFF pipeline
  1. 01 · Pair

    Real frame and synthetic twin

    A camera frame from the real world and its matched rendering from a simulator or generative model.

  2. 02 · Freeze

    Same frozen model

    Both images go through the same system under test, unchanged, so any difference comes from the data.

  3. 03 · Explain

    Decisive evidence

    Counterfactual explanations find the image regions that actually decide each output.

  4. 04 · Compare

    Evidence agreement

    DFF scores how well the two sets of evidence agree; C-DFF breaks the gap down by scene concept.
